Welcome — we're glad you're here

If your relationship with food feels out of your control, you're not alone. Many of us came to our first meeting feeling exactly how you might feel now. Here are answers to the questions newcomers ask most.

  1. What is OA?

    Overeaters Anonymous is a fellowship of people who, through shared experience, strength, and hope, are recovering from compulsive eating and food behaviors. We use the Twelve Steps adapted from Alcoholics Anonymous as our program of recovery.

    OA is not a diet club. We don't weigh in, prescribe a food plan, or charge dues. Whatever your relationship with food — compulsive overeating, undereating, food addiction, anorexia, bulimia, binge eating, or overexercising — you're welcome.

    Our primary purpose is to abstain from compulsive eating and to carry the message of recovery to those who still suffer.

  2. Is OA right for me?

    Only you can answer that. Below are 15 questions adapted from OA's literature. They aren't a diagnosis — just a way to think about whether your eating may be compulsive.

    1. How do I start?

      The simplest start is to attend a meeting. You don't need to introduce yourself, share, or commit to anything — you can just listen.

      • Find a DC metro meeting — in-person, hybrid, virtual, or phone.
      • Look for a newcomer meeting — designed as an introduction with time for questions.
      • Call or text (202) 681-4056 — a real OA member will answer.

      If a meeting doesn't feel like a fit, try another. Meetings vary in tone, format, and focus — it's normal to attend several before one clicks.

    2. What can I expect from a meeting?

      Most meetings last 60 to 90 minutes and follow a simple format:

      • Readings from OA literature — usually the Steps, the Traditions, and a short welcome to newcomers.
      • Members sharing what's working for them and what they're struggling with.
      • A pause to pass the basket — that's the Seventh Tradition (we're self-supporting through our own contributions). You don't need to give.
      • Time at the end for newcomer questions or one-on-one conversation.

      You can listen the whole time. You can leave whenever. You only use your first name. Anonymity is the spiritual foundation of our program — what you hear stays in the room.

    3. How does OA work?

      OA's program of recovery is built on the Twelve Steps, adapted from Alcoholics Anonymous. Working the Steps — usually with a sponsor's guidance — helps members find freedom from the compulsion to eat.

      We also use nine Tools of Recovery that support daily living: a plan of eating, sponsorship, meetings, telephone, writing, literature, an action plan, anonymity, and service.

      You don't need to understand any of this on day one. Members say what helped most was simply keep coming back.

    4. How do I get a sponsor?

      A sponsor is a fellow OA member with some experience working the Steps who agrees to walk you through them. Sponsorship is free and is one of the most powerful Tools.

      • Listen at meetings for someone whose recovery you'd like — what they say, how they handle hard things.
      • Ask after the meeting, or send a message. Most members are honored to be asked.
      • If they can't sponsor, they'll often suggest someone who can. It's normal to ask several people before finding a fit.

      There's no single right way. A sponsor doesn't need to live nearby — phone and video work well.

    5. Where can I call or email with questions?

      You can always reach a real OA member through DC Metro Intergroup. Anonymity is protected — whatever you share is held in confidence.

      • Phone or text: (202) 681-4056
      • Meeting list questions: whereandwhen@oa-dcmetro.org
      • Email reminders: Subscribe to meeting updates

      In a crisis, please call 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline). OA is a fellowship of recovery, not a substitute for medical or mental health care.

    6. Recommended literature

      OA-approved literature is the best companion to meetings. Most members start with one of these:

      • The OA Newcomer's Pamphlet — short, free download from OA.org.
      • Overeaters Anonymous, Second Edition — our main text, with stories of recovery.
      • The Twelve Steps and Twelve Traditions of Overeaters Anonymous.
      • For Today — a book of daily readings.
      • Voices of Recovery — another daily reader, drawn from members' shares.
